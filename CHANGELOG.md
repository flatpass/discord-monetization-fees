## 2026-08-24 — Discord's two rates don't share a base

| Date | Platform | Previously stated | Corrected to | Source | Found by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-24 | Discord Server Subscriptions | **16%** all-in on desktop and browser — 10% Platform Fee plus 6% payment processing, added on one base | **15.4%.** The two rates have different bases. Payment Processing Fees are "calculated as a percentage of the total payments from a user" — 6% of gross. The Platform Fee is a percentage of those payments "less applicable transaction taxes, Payment Processing Fees, and Transaction Fees", so its 10% applies to the 94% that remains and is 9.4% of a sale. 0.06 + 0.10 × 0.94 = 0.154. On a $10 sale: $0.60 processing, then $0.94, seller keeps $8.46. The "90/10 split" phrasing stays accurate — it is 10% of the remainder — but the 10 and the 6 must never be joined by a "plus" | the Fees section of the Monetization Terms, above Schedule 1, support.discord.com — read in a browser 2026-08-24 | maintainer, re-reading the prose that defines the bases the rate table omits |

The 2026-08-03 correction above found the 6% that every secondary guide drops, which was the larger error and still stands. What it missed was one level up the page: **Schedule 1 lists the rates but not what each is a percentage of.** That is stated in the Fees prose above it, and adding two rates without checking their bases produced a number wrong by 0.6 points for three weeks.

`data/fees.json` now carries `"pct_base": "net_of_processing"` on Discord's row — the only row with the field — so the dataset can go on recording the rate the terms state (10%) while anything computing from it applies that 10% to the right base. A row without the field is a percentage of the gross sale.
